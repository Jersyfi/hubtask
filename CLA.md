## 1. Why this agreement exists

Hubtask is published under the Business Source License 1.1, separately for each version, and
every version converts to Apache-2.0 three years after its release; parts of the repository — the
SDKs, the API contract, the n8n package — are Apache-2.0 outright. Two things depend on the
Licensor holding sufficient rights in every line of the codebase:

* **The conversion to Apache-2.0 must actually happen.** If a contribution could not be
  relicensed, the promised conversion would be impossible for that file, and the promise would
  be worthless.
* **Commercial licences must be grantable.** From Licensing Start
  ([ADR-0059](docs/adr/ADR-0059-licensing-phases-and-licensing-start.md)), companies buy a
  licence to the whole product. That requires the right to license all of it, not just the parts
  the Licensor wrote.

A CLA is a real cost — it is friction, it excludes people whose employers will not sign, and it
concentrates power with the Licensor. We chose it deliberately over the alternative, and the
trade-off is recorded in [ADR-0013](docs/adr/ADR-0013-licensing.md). What you get in exchange is
the Change Date: an enforceable commitment in the licence text itself that your work becomes
Apache-2.0 on a fixed schedule, whatever happens to the project or to the Licensor — and the
Licensor's commitment in section 5 that it is never taken back.
