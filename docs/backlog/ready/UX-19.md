# UX-19 — AI where its subject is

**Task:** UX-19 · issue #1651 · `docs/backlog/milestone-UX.md`
**Checked against:** `origin/main` at `5f2562f02` on 2026-10-21; UX-03 read from
`origin/ux-03-reason-never-moves` at `05c020c32` (#1665, open); UX-21 (#1669) and UX-23 (#1675)
read from their branches (open)
**Verdict:** ready

Today the entry's AI sits behind one *Ask AI* menu at the top of the main column. The menu offers
six kinds, a translation panel sits beside it, and both are drawn only for people who may change
the entry. Every proposal lands in one strip, and an empty strip still says "Nothing has been
suggested". UC-AI-04 checks 6–9 ask for four things. Each kind is offered where its subject is.
Duplicates are named by title and offered while an entry is being created. Translation and the
discussion summary are offered to every reader. A running question can be stopped, and a nearly
spent budget is said before asking.

Most of that is client work. Five server facts stand in the way:

1. Every AI request about an entry is authorised at the **workspace** scope. A person whose role
   sits on a hub, a collection or the entry itself is refused, although the strip is drawn for them
   (D3).
2. The thread summary asks for what writing asks for (D4).
3. A field suggestion cannot be asked for one field (D5).
4. No read finds duplicates of words that are not yet an entry (D6).
5. Nobody but an administrator may stop a job, and a stopped AI job still records its proposal (D8).

Nothing in it is on the owner's list. UX-03, UX-21 and UX-23 change the same files and land
first (D13).

**(d)** Standing rules:
- **Rules**: 1 (no domain change); 2 (every new authorisation in the application layer; no adapter
  decides); 3 (the new statement runs in the tenant transaction); 4 (no clock in the domain; the
  budget's day from `quota.StartOfDay`); 5 (no goroutine); 7 (the lookalike read is bounded at
  800 ms and the client aborts superseded reads); 8 (only codes); 9 (the vector statement is a
  parameterised constant like `nearEmbeddings`, which already lives outside sqlc because pgvector is
  detected rather than demanded, ADR-0050; the job's asker read is a sqlc query); 10 (no title in
  any log, metric or audit entry: the duplicates read logs nothing); 11 (contract first, step 2);
  13; 15 (`ai.*` tokens, no literal).
- **P-05**: every ✦ item exists only where the server would answer it (role, type, comments,
  choices, budget); *Apply* and *Dismiss* only for writers; *Stop* only for the asker's own running
  job, which is the only job the client knows the id of.
- **P-03**: nothing deleted; a stopped question stores nothing it had not stored.
- **Known traps**: *`Handler.Invoke` skips validation*, so every refusal test goes through
  `registry.Invoke` or the router. *A write on a refusal path is rolled back*, so the refusal
  entries for the path authorisation are written before the transactions, as `Ask.queue` already
  does. *The registry refuses an undeclared input key*, so `fields` is declared on the descriptor
  and a contract test sends it. *A capability test can ask itself*, so the budget test seeds metered
  tokens and asserts the literal state, not the guard's own answer. *The integration tests share
  one database*: own tenants. *Splitting a screen breaks what pointed into it*: grep `data-tour`,
  `data-ai` and every removed `app.suggestions.*` code. *A live region created already filled is
  not announced*: the existing announcer is kept. *A count needs the plural form*: the lookalike
  line has no count.
- **Two writers at once**: two people asking about one entry keep separate jobs and both proposals
  stand (as today). A stop and a record meet on the job row. The producer takes the row's lock
  (`Queue.Hold`, fenced on the lease) in the transaction that records the proposal, and the cancel's
  statement changes only a job that has not finished. If the cancel commits first, the hold finds
  no row it owns and nothing is recorded. If the record commits first, the proposal stands: the
  stopper then reads `CANCELLED` (the job was still `RUNNING`) or `409 jobs.already_finished` (the
  runner completed it). The client reads the listing after any stop, so it shows what stands in
  either order (D8).

- [x] D8 **The person who asked may watch and stop their question** — decided by: worker, from
  check 9 and `Job.go:81-88`'s own stated condition; restated by the coordinator's ruling on F3 and
  F5.
  - **Who**: `GetJob` and `CancelJob` first read the job's `kind`, `asked_by` and `target_id` with a
    new sqlc query that names `tenant_id = $tenant` itself (not only through row level security), in
    a read-only transaction. Where the kind is one a person asks (`ai.suggest` and the kinds
    UX-23 adds; an arrival proposal is the system's) and `asked_by` is the actor, they ask
    `Permits(READ, [tenant, item(target_id)])`, which records nothing. If that holds, the actor is
    let through without `Authorize`, so nobody let through leaves a DENIED entry. Otherwise the
    existing `Authorize` (`READ` / `STRUCTURE` at the workspace) decides and records as today.
  - A rule's question is asked as the rule's `run_as` account (`RunRule.go:1026`), so that account
    is its `asked_by` and may stop it. An asker who has lost read on the entry falls through to
    `STRUCTURE`.
  - **What a stop does**:
    - A question stopped **before its proposal is recorded** stores nothing. The producer takes the
      job's row lock (`Queue.Hold`, the fence every detached pass uses) in the transaction that
      records the proposal. When the job is no longer its own, that transaction rolls back with
      `queue.lease_lost`. `Produce` gets a `Hold func(ctx) error` from the handler; nil holds
      nothing, as for a direct call.
    - A question stopped **after its proposal is recorded** answers `CANCELLED` (the job was still
      `RUNNING` until the runner completed it), and its proposal stands. Once completed, it answers
      `409 jobs.already_finished`.
    - The contract sentence on `/jobs/{id}:cancel` says both.
  - **A stopped job ends quietly**: the runner maps `queue.lease_lost` to an outcome of its own.
    That means one info-level log line with the code and identifiers, no `JobFailed` and no
    dead-letter signal, no `Queue.Fail`, and no WARN. `Runner_test` proves it.
  - The tokens a provider already spent stay spent, and the budget counts them (UC-AI-07).
  - **Client**: the asking state keeps `jobId`. The pending card's *Stop* posts `:cancel`. The
    state then ends quietly, without the *slow* note, and the listing is read once after any stop
    (`200` or `409`), so whatever stands is shown.
  - A translation is a request: its *Stop* aborts it (`MutateOptions.signal`, new, passed to the
    transport). The server's request context then ends the provider call, because `Translate.go`'s
    bounded context derives from it. The audit entry stays, because content may have been sent.
