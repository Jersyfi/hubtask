**(d)** Rule 2: the ceiling of #1431 is `access.Service.Authorize` with `Grants`; the role at
stake is read through `access.Service.RoleOf` (the authoriser), never by the use case. The guard
is an invariant of the workspace, not an access decision: it counts holders, decides nothing about
the actor, and lives in the identity use cases. Rule 3: the new queries run in the tenant
transaction. Rule 4: no clock. Rule 10: identifiers only. Rule 11: contract first. P-05: *Revoke*
and *New start password* are drawn by the conditions the server refuses on, computed from what the
screen already reads (the workspace's memberships, the groups' members, the accounts' status and
kind); where the client cannot know (an INVITED account it does not list, a race) the refusal's
sentence explains. Known traps: *a write on a refusal path is rolled back* — the ceiling is asked
before the write transaction; *`Handler.Invoke` skips validation* — tests call `Execute`; *a
read-then-write without a lock* — **two writers**: two owners each revoking the other's `OWNER`
(or one revoking, one taking somebody out of an owners group) both see two owners. Guarded by a
per-workspace transaction-level advisory lock (`LockOwners`, the pattern of `legal_hold:` and
`audit_log:`) that every door ending a workspace `OWNER` takes inside its write transaction before
it reads the owners again; the second waits and sees one owner, and is refused. Lock order: a group
change takes the group's row (#1421), then the owners' lock; a revocation takes only the owners'
lock; nothing takes them the other way round, so no deadlock. A grant of `OWNER` takes no owners'
lock: missing a grant committed meanwhile can only refuse wrongly, never let the last owner go.
The guard is asked once read-only before the step-up (a proof is not burnt on a refused act) and
again under the lock (the decision). The start-password race (`OWNER` granted to the account
between the check and the write) needs an owner's own act and is accepted.

## 3. Decisions

- [x] D1 The rule: the workspace keeps at least one owner — a door that would end the last
  counting owner's `OWNER` on the workspace is refused with `409 memberships.last_owner` —
  decided by: worker, after #1429's proposal; P-16's spirit ("a door that would leave somebody with
  no way in refuses") applied to administration. Written in `domain-model.md` §3.2.
- [x] D2 Who counts: a person (`USER`) whose account is `ACTIVE` or `RESTRICTED` (restriction is not
  a lockout) and not deleted, holding `OWNER` at the workspace directly or through a group — decided
  by: worker. Rejected: counting `INVITED` (an invitation may never be redeemed, leaving nobody),
  service accounts (nobody signs in as one to administer the workspace).
- [x] D3 Refuse only a change that **ends** the last owner: where no counting owner existed before,
  nothing is refused — decided by: worker; a workspace imported without one would otherwise freeze
  every owner-grant cleanup.
- [x] D4 Group joins count: a member change that takes out every owner but adds a counting person
  to a group holding workspace `OWNER` passes — decided by: worker; replacing the owners group's
  members is a normal handover.
- [x] D5 *Groups* shows the refusal's sentence instead of hiding member removal and deletion for the
  last owners' group — decided by: worker. The screen reads no workspace memberships or account
  states and which removal would leave nobody depends on the member chosen; P-05's "the screen says
  why" holds through the code's sentence. *People*, which reads both, hides *Revoke*.
- [x] D6 Out of scope: an erasure (Art. 17) of the last owner is not refused by this guard — an
  erasure is the controller's legal duty, carried out by an owner or the operator, and refusing it
  is not a product rule a worker adds — decided by: worker; reported as a finding.
- [x] D7 The step-up for groups (#1430): a member change (a changed list) or a deletion of a group
  holding `OWNER` at any scope demands the proof, as granting or revoking `OWNER` does at any
  scope — decided by: identity.md §16.3. The decision reads the roles the ceiling asked about; a
  role added after is the existing `groups.roles_changed`. The proof travels as on every other
  route, the optional `X-Hubtask-Step-Up` header (`components/parameters/StepUpToken`), which is
  additive.
- [x] D8 The start-password ceiling (#1431): the actor must be allowed to grant the target's
  effective role on the workspace (`RoleOf` at the workspace, then `Authorize` with `Grants`) —
  decided by: worker after #1431's proposal and `domain-model.md` §3.2. Rejected: every role at
  every scope — a managed member of a private hub would then be refused by the hub's privacy with a
  misleading code, which is a question of its own (reported as a finding: a start password reaches
  whatever the account reaches, a private hub included). PH-07 D4's premise is no longer true; the
  record is a snapshot and stays, identity.md §13 states the new rule.
- [x] D9 No ADR — the three are consequences of existing rules (§3.2's ceiling, §16.3's list,
  P-16) — decided by: worker.
