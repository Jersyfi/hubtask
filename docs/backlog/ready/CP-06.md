## 3. Decisions

- [x] D1 **What is re-applied: the data subject erasures completed after the recovery point** — the
  cases of kind `ERASURE`, `COMPLETED` at the end of the archive with `completed_at` after the point.
  Not the other journalled removals (`USER`, `RETENTION`, `ADMIN`): a recovery point is chosen
  "before the damage", and a mass deletion is often that damage; retention deletes again by itself
  on its next pass. Decided by: worker; §8.5 step 5 and data-protection.md §5 say "erasures". The
  narrower reading removes less, so it is not an irrecoverable deletion the owner has to decide;
  the wider one would be.
- [x] D2 **The case is re-run, not its effects replayed**: the same `Eraser` runs again, under the
  holds the recovered workspace has once step 5's first half placed them, with the action
  `dsr.erasure_reapplied` (counts, mode, the recovery point). A hold placed in the rewound period
  therefore keeps what it covers, as it would have kept it had it come first. Decided by: worker;
  #1247 (the holds first) and the milestone's acceptance.
- [x] D3 **The subject of a full deletion is found where the erasure left it**: `hubctl`'s reader
  takes `subject_account_id` where the case still names it, and otherwise the account the erasure
  removed — the `deletion_journal` row (`account`, `DSR_ERASURE`) whose `deleted_at` is the
  moment of the case's `dsr.erased` entry (the erasure writes both with one clock reading). Decided
  by: worker. Rejected: keeping the subject on a completed case (changes what the register holds, a
  privacy design); pairing by nearest time (two erasures close together would swap people). The
  match is exact and unique: where two journal rows match one entry, or none does, the reader
  sends nothing for that case and reports it (`SUBJECT_AMBIGUOUS`, `SUBJECT_UNRESOLVED`) — never a
  guess. Coordinator's condition, 2026-10-15.
- [x] D4 **Two writers**: the completion is conditional (`status <> 'COMPLETED'`); the erasure
  already holds the case's lock. The use case does not stop the rewound job; whichever completes
  first wins, the other answers `PRESENT` / no-ops. Decided by: worker; known trap.
- [x] D8 **A step-up, declared on the descriptor**: the operation deletes data irrecoverably from
  input the operator supplies, so it demands a fresh re-authentication like backup-restore.md §8's
  destructive modes; identity.md §16.3's list names it. `hubctl` proves it with `proveAgain`, as
  `admin tenant delete` does. Decided by: the coordinator's review, 2026-10-15; identity.md §16.3.
- [x] D9 **What stops a crafted file from erasing a person who never asked.** The reader takes rows
  only from the temporary cluster (or a file the operator names), never from the request. The server
  re-checks what the recovered workspace can say:
  - A case it holds must be an erasure of this workspace, with the same mode and subject, not yet
    completed or rejected.
  - A case it lacks must have been received after the recovery point (else the rewind could not
    have lost it), and completed after the point and before now.
  - The erasure runs in the workspace's scope, so a subject from elsewhere is absent.

  What stays trusted is the operator holding `admin:tenants` with a fresh step-up, the same trust
  that can already request a workspace's deletion. Every re-run is in the workspace's trail with
  the case. Decided by: the coordinator's review, 2026-10-15; worker for the checks.
- [x] D5 **No archive fallback for erasures**: an archive carries neither the cases nor the
  pseudonyms nor the journal, and its trail does not name the person. Where the end of the archive
  is unusable, the procedure says the erasures of the rewound period cannot be re-run from an
  archive, and the workspace's administrator lists them from the trail (`dsr.erased` after the
  point) and records them again. Decided by: worker; the archive's exclusions are themselves
  decided (`Record.go`). The milestone's "(or the archive fallback)" is corrected in the same pull
  request.
- [x] D6 **Cases not completed at the end of the archive, other kinds, and a workspace deletion
  requested in the rewound period are not this task.** A rewind loses them from the register (an
  open erasure with its deadline, a restriction under Art. 18, an access request answered) — a gap
  with its own shape (re-place a case without carrying it out), filed as finding #1515; a
  workspace deletion requested after the point is filed in the same finding. Decided by: worker; the
  task, #1271 and check 3 name completed erasures.
- [x] D7 **The source is the temporary cluster or a file**, read by `hubctl` as for the holds
  (`--from-cluster`, `--from`); the server takes the cases with the request, at most 1,000 per call.
  Decided by: worker; `ReplaceLegalHolds`.

## 4. Proof and steps

Proof:

- **Integration, through the registry, as the application role:** a workspace with three accounts.
  Two erasures are completed (one full deletion, one anonymisation) and one hold is placed. The
  database is then "rewound" by restoring the rows as they stood (the accounts and their content
  back, the cases missing or `IN_PROGRESS`). The use case then runs with the cases as the reader
  answers them. The test asserts:
  - the deleted account is gone again;
  - the anonymised one reads "former user";
  - the case reads `COMPLETED` at its original moment;
  - the trail holds `dsr.erasure_reapplied`;
  - a second run answers `PRESENT` and changes nothing;
  - the account under the hold is restricted, not erased;
  - another workspace is untouched.
- **Reader test against a real database** (the temporary cluster's stand-in): it finds the subject
  of a full deletion through the journal and the trail entry, and reports the ambiguous and the
  unresolved case without a subject. It also leaves out cases completed
  before the point, open cases and other kinds.
- **Unit tests:** the outcomes `SUBJECT_ABSENT`, `BLOCKED`, `CONFLICT` and a refused batch.
- **Registry tests:** the step-up refusal, each mismatch of D9, and the input refusals.
- **`hubctl` tests:** the flags, one source, the table, and batching.
