Navigation concept · Version 3 · 7 Nov 2026

Hubtask Kompass

One frame for all products. Every place in the navigation answers exactly one question, and every product gets there exactly the navigation its things need.

What version 3 changes

  • The basis is now an inventory of the code for all nine products: which objects exist, who owns them (workspace, hub, collection, person), how they nest and are ordered, which operations exist and what they refuse. A second piece of research adds how the leading suites show each product inside a space (§2, §3).
  • The hub level is organised anew, by the kind of thing and no longer by product name. At the top stand the hub tools (Queues with live counters, Plan, Inventory, Time, Goals), then the collections and then one mixed content tree for pages and whiteboards (§4).
  • Constellations: for every template in the catalogue, the hub level is shown, from the household to the large IT operations (§5).
  • Create, order, move for every thing: hubs, collections, pages, whiteboards, queues, products in the hub. This includes the pre-check when changing hubs and the new interfaces it needs (§6).

1 · The finding in one paragraph

Today every product exists twice, as a global home and as a hub tab. In a hub, “Pages” stands on the screen three times at once, and up to five navigation levels lie on top of each other. The wiki draws its own tree box, the assets a second tab bar. On the phone two rows of tabs scroll sideways. The products were built side by side, without a shared model. Version 1 described that. This version builds the model from the products’ actual data.

4 · The hub level

Whoever opens a hub sees the hub in the sidebar. It has three parts in a fixed order. Each part is built after the kind of thing that stands in it:

Part 1 · Hub toolsOne entry per tool that is switched onQueues (Service desk) expand to their queues with live counters. Plan, Inventory, Time and Goals each open a page with tabs of their own. The order is fixed, so that every hub looks the same. The tools stand at the top because otherwise they disappear under a long tree (the complaint about Confluence in 2025).
Part 2 · CollectionsThe hub’s work listsTask lists, boards, Scrum teams and desks, ordered by hand. A mark says what the collection is: desk, runs in sprints. The figure shows what counts: “Sprint 42 · 6 d”, “1 SLA”, “12 open”. Views are never children in the sidebar, but tabs in the header.
Part 3 · ContentsOne tree for pages and whiteboardsAs in Confluence: whiteboards may lie under pages, but have no children themselves. A filter with “All · Pages · Whiteboards” and the view modes tree, recently changed and A–Z (personal). Dragging works only in the tree.

Above it, For you (Today, Inbox, Waiting for your yes) always stays fixed; below it, Administration, Archive, Trash always stay fixed. Between the two lies the hub header: “‹ All hubs”, the hub mark with the name and ▾ for the quick hub switch, ☆ and ⋯. The hub’s settings are only in the ⋯. A part appears only when its product is on in the hub. If it is empty, it appears only for people who may create something, and then shows a quiet line “Create the first page”.

The content tree in detail

  • Pages nest up to 12 levels (server limit), at most 5,000 per hub. The indent grows up to level 6. 15 siblings per level, then “n more”.
  • Whiteboards stand in the same tree, at the top or under a page, but without children of their own. Existing boards have no place yet. After the change they stand on the top level after the pages, in alphabetical order, until someone files them.
  • Filter from 30 entries on, sticks at the top: free text and “All · Pages · Whiteboards”. Matches appear with their parents.
  • View modes in the section’s ⋯, personal: tree (default, draggable), recently changed, A–Z. “All contents” opens the table with search and sorting.
  • Templates (pages marked as a template) do not stand in the tree, but in the create dialogue.

6 · Create, order, move

The same grammar holds for every thing. ＋ on the section header creates on the top level, ＋ on a row creates beneath it, ⋯ on a row manages. Dragging orders within a hub. Another hub can be reached only through “Move to…” with a pre-check, never by dragging, because access and types change on the way. What the data cannot do is not offered.

6.2 · Try dragging (mixed content tree)

Drag by the grip or by the row. Top quarter: before. Middle: inside. Bottom quarter: after. Dragging to the left below the last child outdents. Hovering over a collapsed page expands it after 600 ms. Esc cancels. A whiteboard takes nothing in and says why, as does a page dragged into itself. With the keyboard: focus a row, Alt+↑↓ moves, Alt+→← indents and outdents. ⌘Z takes it back.

6.3 · Into another hub: a pre-check instead of an error message

Today the server refuses a change of hub when the name is already taken in the target (containers.name_taken), when entries use a type of the old hub (entry_types.hub_bound) or when the collection runs a workflow of the old hub (workflows.hub_bound). The dialogue checks this before the click. It shows every obstacle with the one step that resolves it. Only when everything is green does “Move” become possible. The flow is in the mockup under “Collection to another hub”.

What you decide with the approval

  • UX-1 changes: a hub shows its products as three parts in the sidebar (tools, collections, contents), no longer as tabs. The hub overview no longer has tabs.
  • Whiteboards move into the content tree beside the pages (new fields and a new operation, purely additive).
  • The hub tab “Desk” becomes “Queues” in the sidebar, with live counters.
  • A collection’s “Plan” and “Timeline” become one view; a hub’s timeline is the tool “Plan”.
  • New interfaces: Starred, whiteboard place, pre-check when moving, queue counters (all additive).
  • Tools in the hub have a fixed order; one switches them off in the hub, not personally.

Deliberately not in this step, because the server cannot do it: moving pages or whiteboards into another hub, grouping hubs into folders, dragging several pages at once. Each of these would be a step of its own.
