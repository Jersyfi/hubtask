# SC-15, D5 — terms of use at a provider for consumers (a supplement)

**When:** 2026-10-13 · **Commit:** `d28d57d8` (branch `claude/sc-15-walk`, with `origin/main` merged
in, which brings the terms of use)
**Kind:** using ([README](./README.md), "How to walk"). This supplements
[`SC-15-D5-2026-10-13.md`](./SC-15-D5-2026-10-13.md), which did not walk the terms of use because
they were not on the branch yet. The server was built with `go build` from a private copy of that
tree, for Linux, with the web app (`apps/webapp/dist`, built from the same tree) embedded. It ran in
a container so that its trust store could hold the walk's own throwaway certificate authority
(`SSL_CERT_FILE`); on macOS a Go binary trusts only the system keychain, and the product demands an
`https` issuer. The settings:
- **multi mode** (`HUBTASK_TENANCY_MODE=multi`), every role, `HUBTASK_BASE_URL=http://hubtask.localhost:18545`,
  so each workspace answers at `<short name>.hubtask.localhost:18545`;
- an encryption keyring, `HUBTASK_RATE_LIMIT_BURST=600`, no SMTP, so an invitation answered its link;
- **`HUBTASK_HTTP_ALLOW_PRIVATE_NETWORKS=true`**, because the fake issuer ran on this machine
  (`https://host.docker.internal:18546`).

The database was its own PostgreSQL 16 (pgvector) container, migrated from empty to 0140 with
`cmd/migrate`, and the server reached it as `hubtask_app`, so row level security was in force. The
browser was Chromium through Playwright, light theme, English, at 1280 × 900 and at 375 × 812 (a
phone with touch). Chromium mapped `host.docker.internal` to the loopback address and accepted the
throwaway certificate.

**The installation:** set up with `scripts/dev-workspace.sh --bootstrap` from the printed setup
code: the provider's workspace "Provider operations" (`ops`) and its owner and operator Olga
(`olga@provider.example`). With the `admin:tenants` token the bootstrap minted, the same script
provisioned two consumer workspaces owned by Anna (`anna@consumer.example`): "Anna's tasks"
(`anna`) and "Anna's book club" (`anna-club`). Anna invited Carl, then Bert, into `anna` through
`POST /accounts:invite`. Nora arrived through a provider. Every account is a throwaway on a database
that no longer exists. The presses, the measured values, the database and the fake issuer's log are
in [`SC-15-D5-terms-2026-10-13/walk.log`](./SC-15-D5-terms-2026-10-13/walk.log).

**Use case checks it shows:**
- UC-ID-19 check 5: before any terms existed, the invitation card had no box and no link, and the
  next sign-in had no step.
- UC-ID-19 check 1 and UC-ID-07 check 4: with the installation's terms, version 3, the invitation is
  not accepted without the box. The account then holds the link, the version and the time.
- UC-ID-19 check 2: the operator raised the version to 4. Bert, who had agreed to 3, met the step
  at his next sign-in before any session opened. Carl and Olga, who had agreed to none, met it too.
- UC-ID-19 check 3: declining opened no session, and the card said whom to ask: "whoever provides
  this service to you".
- UC-ID-19 check 4: the trail of `anna` records each agreement with the link, the version and
  `terms_set_by INSTALLATION`. It records no text.
- UC-ID-19 check 6: a provider sign-in through a local fake issuer met the same step after the
  return, before the session opened. Declining it opened no session; agreeing opened one.

The screenshots are in [`SC-15-D5-terms-2026-10-13/`](./SC-15-D5-terms-2026-10-13/).

## What was pressed

- In a shell:
  - `scripts/dev-workspace.sh --bootstrap` with the printed code, and twice more to provision
    `anna` and `anna-club`.
  - `POST /accounts:invite` as Anna, for Carl and later for Bert. No mail was sent, so each answer
    carried the link.
  - For the provider set-up, as Anna:
    - Anna's sign-in owed version 4, and she agreed through `POST /auth/sessions:agree-terms`;
    - `POST /identity-providers` with a step-up: GENERIC, issuer
      `https://host.docker.internal:18546`, *Anyone this provider knows* (`ANY`), newcomers get
      `MEMBER`, and a made-up client id and secret;
    - `POST /identity-providers/{id}:offer` with `offered: true`, with a step-up.

  These are set-up only; the provider walks in D4.
- In the browser, every press a click, with the focus read before it (walk.log):
  - Carl: *Set the password and sign in*, *Skip the tour*, then *Sign in*.
  - Olga (twice): *Sign in*, the proof's password and *Prove it*, *Change these values*, the
    *Terms* link, *Workspaces may: not change it* and *Version of the terms* (3, then 4), and
    *Save*. The second time she also pressed *Agree and continue* on her own step.
  - Anna: *Sign in*, *Agree and continue* and *Skip the tour*. Later, on *The trail*, she typed
    `auth.terms` into *What was done* and pressed *Look*.
  - Bert:
    - *Set the password and sign in* without the box;
    - the box, then *Set the password and sign in*, then *Skip the tour*;
    - *Sign in* and *Decline*, at 1280 and at 375;
    - *Sign in* and *Agree and continue*.
  - Nora: *Sign in with Consumer ID (walk issuer)* and the fake issuer's *Continue as
    nora@consumer.example*, then *Decline* (375). The second time (1280) she pressed the same two,
    then *Agree and continue* and *Skip the tour*.

No key was pressed in the browser except typing into fields.

## What was seen

- **The database after the walk** (owner role, walk.log):
  - `account`, for those who agreed, holds the link, the version and a time:
    - Anna and Bert in `anna`: `https://provider.example/legal/terms`, version `4`;
    - Nora in `anna`: the same link, version `4`;
    - Olga in `ops`: the same link, version `3`. She has not signed in since version 4.
  - Carl agreed to nothing, so his columns are empty, and Anna in `anna-club` never signed in there.
  - Carl's password sign-in after version 4, through the API, answered `202` with
    `methods ["TERMS"]` and `terms {set_by INSTALLATION, url …, version "4"}`, and no session.
  - `instance_setting` holds `legal.terms_url` and `legal.terms_version` "4", both locked.
- **The trail** has six `auth.terms_agreed` entries: five in `anna` and one in `ops`. Each row's
  `changes` holds exactly three fields: `terms_url`, `terms_version` and `terms_set_by`. Its
  `context` holds no text. Bert's version-3 agreement shares its moment with his
  `auth.invitation_redeemed`, so activation and agreement were one transaction.
- **Declining sends nothing.** On the three declines (Bert twice, Nora once), no
  `/auth/sessions:agree-terms` request and no session token were seen, and the database counts no
  session for them.
- **The fake issuer** logged the server's discovery, two `GET /authorize` (`response_type=code`,
  `scope=openid profile email`, `code_challenge_method=S256`, no `prompt`, no `max_age`,
  `redirect_uri=http://hubtask.localhost:18545/auth/callback`), and per return one `POST /token`
  whose PKCE verifier matched, then `GET /jwks`.
- **Every card stayed inside 375 px** (scrollWidth 375 at 375) and showed no script error. The 403s
  in the log are Carl's, Bert's and Nora's reads of screens their role does not reach. Carl and Bert
  were invited with no role.

## What was not walked

- **A real identity provider** (milestone SC Decision 9). Check 6 was walked with a local fake
  issuer, which vouched for `nora@consumer.example`. No real provider was called, and no real
  account was used.
- **An invitation accepted through a provider with the box ticked.** The provider's start carries
  the agreement beside the invitation token. That case is proven by
  `apps/webapp/e2e/terms.test.mjs` (the box before *Contoso Entra ID*) and
  `core/application/service/identity/TermsInvitation_test.go`. It was not walked here.
- **Terms that a workspace sets for itself, under an open installation lock.** SC-10 walked them in
  single mode. Here the installation locked its terms, as a provider for consumers would.
- **A second installation without terms.** Check 5 was shown on this one, before the operator set
  its terms.
- **The terms changing while a step is open** (`auth.terms_changed`).

## What was found

1. **"Signing in as" is empty after a provider's return.** On the terms step that follows the
   provider (`13-…`, both widths), the line reads "Signing in as" with no address, followed by
   "Not you?". After a password it names the address (`5-…`, `10-…`). Nora cannot see which account
   she is about to agree for. → [#1336](https://github.com/Jersyfi/hubtask/issues/1336)
2. **The invitation link and the provider's return land on the installation's base host, not on the
   workspace's.**
   - The invitation link `POST /accounts:invite` answers is
     `http://hubtask.localhost:18545/redeem#…`. The card reads "to hubtask.localhost:18545" although
     it says "Join Anna's tasks".
   - After accepting, Carl and Bert stay on `hubtask.localhost`.
   - The provider's `redirect_uri` is the base host too, so Nora's terms step reads "to
     hubtask.localhost:18545". After her decline, the card she is left on is the base host's
     sign-in, not `anna`'s.

   The terms walk is not affected, because everything resolved to the right workspace. A consumer
   sees the provider's address where they would expect their workspace's. → [#1337](https://github.com/Jersyfi/hubtask/issues/1337)
3. **A newcomer through a provider is created before the terms step.** After Nora declined, her
   account existed as `ACTIVE` with no session and no agreement (walk.log). Check 3 holds: no
   session opened. The account remains, though, and an administrator sees a person in *People* who
   never agreed. → [#1338](https://github.com/Jersyfi/hubtask/issues/1338)
4. Smaller observations ([#1339](https://github.com/Jersyfi/hubtask/issues/1339)):
   - On the installation's form, *Version of the terms* is listed after *Accessibility*, not next
     to *Terms*.
   - While the form is open, the version's disabled lock still reads "use their own" after *Terms*
     is set to "not change it" (`3-…`). After *Save*, both read "not change it".
   - The story's "The terms changed on 1 November" has no counterpart. The step names the version
     but no date, which no check asks for.
