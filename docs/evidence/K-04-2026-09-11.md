# K-04 — The duplicate threshold, measured

**2026-09-11, local development machine.** [#532](https://github.com/Jersyfi/hubtask/issues/532)
asked for the number K-04 argued for — `suggestion.DefaultDuplicateFloor = 0.9` — to be measured
against a corpus, for the models this product stores, and it asked four questions: where
paraphrases of one piece of work sit, where entries that merely share a subject sit, whether the
band moves between models enough to want a default per model, and whether it moves between
languages. This is the first run. It answers three of the four, leaves the fourth honestly open, and
changes the default.

## Why the two local models are the right ones to measure, as of this branch

They were not, until [#551](https://github.com/Jersyfi/hubtask/issues/551): the index is 1536
dimensions wide and the store took nothing narrower, so a vector from either model failed at insert
and neither could govern a floor the product compares against. Since
[ADR-0054](../adr/ADR-0054-embedding-width.md) a narrower vector is zero-padded, which changes no
cosine similarity — neither the dot product nor either norm — so the numbers here are the numbers
the product computes for these models. This document depends on that branch having landed.

## The corpus, and what it is not

Author-written and **labelled before any similarity was computed**; no label was changed after a
score was seen, and the one place that hurt is recorded below rather than corrected. Every field
name, role and group name is English; the titles and notes are content, in the two languages the
product is used in (rule 13 governs the project's artefacts, not the data under test —
`test/integration/search_test.go` carries German titles for the same reason).

The labels are **groups and roles**, and every pair's class is derived from them by the harness:

| Role | Meaning | Pairs it makes |
|---|---|---|
| `anchor` | the reference piece of work | — |
| `duplicate` | the same work, paraphrased, same language | `duplicate` with the anchor |
| `duplicate-xl` | the same work in the other language | `duplicate-xl` |
| `near-identical` | the anchor with one word or a punctuation mark changed — a control that must sit at the top of the band, or the model is the finding | `duplicate` |
| `related` | the same subject, different work — the **hard band** the floor has to sit above; `twin` names the item it is a translation of | `subject`, and one `duplicate-xl` |
| `namesake` | shares a proper noun with the anchor and nothing else — a control that must sit in the unrelated band | `namesake` |
| `single` | a group of one | `unrelated` with everything |

The `related` items were built adversarially rather than conveniently: same verb, different object
(*Take the cat for her vaccination* / *Take the dog for his vaccination*), same object, different verb
(*Book a dentist appointment* / *Cancel the dentist appointment on Thursday*), the parent-and-child
shape the product's own query excludes (*Prepare the conference talk* / *Book the train to the
conference*), and the near-miss a person would hesitate over (*Fix the dripping kitchen tap* / *Fix the
dripping tap in the bathroom*).

**Prevalence.** 43,365 pairs, of which 75 are same-language duplicates and 167 cross-lingual ones:
about half a percent. That is why nothing below reports a precision — a precision over a balanced
corpus would overstate a workspace's by orders of magnitude — and why the per-item view is the one
that matters: *for each entry, its true duplicate against the closest thing that is not one.*

**Held out.** The 64 groups are split into a tuning half and a reporting half by the parity of the
group name's character sum — decided by the name, not by anything measured. The rule for choosing a
floor was written into the harness before the numbers existed; the numbers below are from the
reporting half unless they say otherwise.

**What it is not.** It is not field material. A corpus one person writes has the paraphrase structure
that person imagines. What it is not, either, is invented vectors: every number here is a model
embedding text anybody can read and disagree with, and the corpus is committed so that anybody can
re-run it, argue with a label, or replace it with real entries.

## The bands

Similarity by pair class, all pairs, both halves. p5 / median / p95.

| Class | pairs | `mxbai-embed-large` | `nomic-embed-text` |
|---|---|---|---|
| same-language duplicate | 75 | 0.68 / 0.88 / 0.98 | 0.65 / 0.85 / 1.00 |
| cross-lingual duplicate | 167 | 0.41 / 0.59 / 0.80 | 0.36 / 0.49 / 0.71 |
| same subject, different work | 553 | 0.39 / 0.59 / 0.77 | 0.35 / 0.54 / 0.77 |
| shares a proper noun only | 34 | 0.41 / 0.47 / 0.78 | 0.31 / 0.48 / 0.76 |
| unrelated | 42536 | 0.31 / 0.42 / 0.63 | 0.31 / 0.39 / 0.61 |

Three things are visible before any floor is discussed.

1. **A same-language paraphrase sits around 0.85–0.89**, not above 0.95. The comment on
   `DefaultDuplicateFloor` said *"two paraphrases of one sentence sit above 0.95"*; that is where the
   `near-identical` controls sit (the top of the band, 0.98–0.995 — the control passed), and it is
   not where a paraphrase sits. The issue's own example pair — *Renew the domain registration* /
   *Renew the domain name before it lapses* — is at 0.853 on `nomic-embed-text`.
2. **The subject band's top overlaps the duplicate band's middle.** Its p95 is 0.77 on both models
   and its maximum 0.92 / 0.90. The pairs up there are the adversarial ones by construction: the two
   taps (0.897 / 0.891), the cat and the dog (0.899 on `nomic-embed-text`), the smoke alarm and the
   remote (0.923 / 0.874). No floor separates *same verb, different object* from *same work,
   reworded* — for these models the two are the same distance apart.
3. **A translation is not a duplicate to either model.** Cross-lingual pairs sit at a median of
   0.59 / 0.49 — inside the subject band, overlapping the unrelated one. Question 4 is answered: the
   product indexes an entry in the language it was written in, and a German entry and its English
   twin are never proposed as each other's duplicate by these two models, at any floor that is not
   also proposing the whole workspace.

The `namesake` control held: a shared proper noun alone sits in the unrelated band (median 0.47 /
0.48), with one exception on `mxbai-embed-large` worth naming — *Fahrrad zur Inspektion bringen* and
*Fahrradanhänger beim Nachbarn abholen* at 0.858, a shared word stem carrying more than it should.
Title-only pairs score lower than pairs with notes in every class; the effect is 0.02–0.05 on the
median for duplicates and the same for subjects, so it moves the bands without moving the gap.

## The per-item view, and the curve

For each entry on the reporting half: would every one of its true same-language duplicates be
proposed at this floor, and would anything that is *not* one be? Counts of entries, not rates.

| Floor | `mxbai` duplicates found | `mxbai` entries with a false one | `nomic` duplicates found | `nomic` entries with a false one | translations found (`mxbai` · `nomic`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.750 | 63/67 | 60/157 | 57/67 | 38/157 | 10/125 · 6/125 |
| 0.775 | 63/67 | 43/157 | 55/67 | 25/157 | 8/125 · 6/125 |
| 0.800 | 61/67 | 23/157 | 53/67 | 12/157 | 6/125 · 6/125 |
| 0.825 | 57/67 | 10/157 | 53/67 | 6/157 | 5/125 · 2/125 |
| 0.850 | 53/67 | 6/157 | 36/67 | 6/157 | 5/125 · 2/125 |
| 0.875 | 45/67 | 2/157 | 33/67 | 1/157 | 0/125 · 0/125 |
| 0.900 | 22/67 | 2/157 | 23/67 | 0/157 | 0/125 · 0/125 |
| 0.925 | 16/67 | 0/157 | 15/67 | 0/157 | 0/125 · 0/125 |

Reading it:

* **At 0.9 the product finds a third of the paraphrases** — 22 of 67 and 23 of 67 — and almost
  nothing false. What it finds at 0.9 is largely the `near-identical` controls: copies, not
  rewordings. That is not the band the constant's comment said it was choosing.
* **0.85 is the knee for both models.** `mxbai-embed-large` finds 53 of 67 with 6 of 157 entries
  shown a false candidate; `nomic-embed-text` finds 36 of 67 with 6 of 157. Every false candidate
  at 0.85 is listed in the next section, and every one of them is a near-miss a person can
  reasonably be asked about.
* **Below 0.825 the false candidates climb faster than the found ones**: at 0.8, 23 and 12 entries
  of 157; at 0.775, 43 and 25. That is the inbox of noise K-04 was written to avoid.
* **The two models differ, and not by enough for two defaults.** At the same floor
  `nomic-embed-text` finds fewer and proposes as many false; its duplicate band is lower by about
  0.03 at the median and its non-duplicate tail is, on the tuning half, higher. One number serves
  both at 0.85 with a different recall, and the difference is smaller than the difference between
  a title-only pair and one with notes. Question 3 is answered: **not a default per model** — and
  the configuration surface could not express one without an ADR, since
  `HUBTASK_AI_DUPLICATE_THRESHOLD` is one scalar.

**The rule written before the numbers said something else.** The harness chose, on the tuning
half, the midpoint between the 5th percentile of an entry's lowest true duplicate and the 95th
percentile of its highest non-duplicate: **0.78** for `mxbai-embed-large` and **0.74** for
`nomic-embed-text`. On the reporting half those floors find nearly every duplicate and show a false
candidate to 43 and 25 entries of 157 — a quarter and a sixth of the workspace. The rule was a
symmetric one and the cost is not symmetric: K-04's own reasoning is that a missed duplicate is
found by searching, which is what people do today, and a false proposal is how a feature stops
being read. The rule is kept in the harness as what was pre-registered; the recommendation departs
from it, and this paragraph is the record of that.

## What this does not measure

* **The product's retrieval.** `Near` in `EmbeddingRepository.go` takes the five nearest, excludes
  the entry's own subtree and the same-model predicate, and the caller then narrows to what the
  reader may see. A false candidate the reader cannot see still occupies one of the five; a subtree
  is the most similar text in a workspace and is excluded. And the HNSW index is approximate
  (`ef_search` 40) where this run's arithmetic is exact. The curve above is an upper bound on what
  an inbox shows, not a prediction of it. Closing that loop — the chosen floor through
  `SuggestDuplicates` against a seeded workspace — is the next run, and it needs the vectors this
  one produced.
* **`text-embedding-3-small`.** No key on this machine. The row is filled by
  `HUBTASK_EVIDENCE_OLLAMA_URL=<an OpenAI-compatible endpoint that speaks Ollama's /api/embed>` —
  or, more honestly, by extending the harness with the `OpenAiCompatible` adapter, which is a
  dozen lines and a key. Until then the default rests on two of the three models the issue named,
  and the third is the one most installations will have configured.
* **Long notes.** Nothing truncates `embeddingText`, so an entry with notes longer than the model's
  context is cut by the model at a point this run never reached; the longest notes here are five
  sentences.
* **The `search_document:` prefix** `nomic-embed-text`'s model card asks for. The product sends
  none, so this run sends none; a band measured with it would be a band the product does not
  compute. If somebody adds the prefix to the adapter, this run is to be repeated.

## What changes

`suggestion.DefaultDuplicateFloor` moves from **0.9 to 0.85**, and its comment stops asserting
bands as fact and points here. `HUBTASK_AI_DUPLICATE_THRESHOLD` stays the operator's override.
The reasoning the constant carried — a false proposal costs more than a missed duplicate — stands,
and is what keeps the number at 0.85 rather than at the pre-registered 0.78.

Not a default per model, for the reason above. Not cross-lingual duplicate detection, because no
floor gives it with these models; the limitation is written into `ai-first.md` §2.

## To repeat this

```bash
docker run -d --name hubtask-embed -p 11434:11434 ollama/ollama
docker exec hubtask-embed ollama pull nomic-embed-text
docker exec hubtask-embed ollama pull mxbai-embed-large
HUBTASK_EVIDENCE_OLLAMA_URL=http://localhost:11434 HUBTASK_EVIDENCE_OUT=/tmp/evidence \
  go test -tags evidence ./test/evidence/ -run TestWhereTheDuplicateBandsSit -v
```

The report is `duplicate-threshold.json` in the output directory, with the digests, the norms, every
band, both halves and the whole curve. Compare the digests first: `latest` moves, and a band that
moved with the model is a different finding from a band that moved with the corpus.
