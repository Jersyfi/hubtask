## QS-27 — A device with a clock three hours out synchronises

B's `hubctl` stamped `1789534140534` (04:49 UTC, three hours ahead); the server recorded the change
under B's device with its own reading, `1789523341032` (01:49 UTC) — bounded, as §4.1 says. Two
seconds later A's correctly-clocked edit won, which a reading three hours ahead would have
prevented for three hours.

```

## QS-26 — A user loses access to a hub while offline

The revocation reaches B's device as one `ACCESS_REVOKED` record at the hub, addressed to Bert;
the creation B queued while away is `REJECTED` with `forbidden`; A, who keeps the hub, is told
nothing.

```

-- B's device is away with a valid cursor and a queued creation

-- B comes back: the pull

## QS-25 — A device ninety-one days away knows an entry deleted in the meantime

The cursor is refused with `sync.cursor_too_old` in the catalogue's words, the full synchronisation
delivers everything current and not the deleted entry, and a cursor one day inside the window is
accepted. The entry is in the trash rather than purged: the walk delivers live rows only, and a
purge is the retention engine's after its period — SY-5 in `test/integration` covers the purged
case with a tombstone.

```
== QS-25: a device ninety-one days away knows an entry deleted in the meantime
-- A creates 'Old note', pulls, and holds it
held: 1 record(s) for 01a0a7e7-e768-7c58-b9a8-2467afc17da1
-- the device's cursor, as a clock the walk controls minted it ninety-one days ago (the installation's own codec)
-- meanwhile 'Old note' is deleted
1
-- the device comes back with its cursor
hubctl: This stream position is older than the offline window, so the changes since then are no longer all kept. Resynchronise in full.
-- so it starts over: the full synchronisation, and 'Old note' is not in it
records: 12; 'Old note' among them: 0
-- a cursor of ninety days, inside the window, is accepted
accepted
```
