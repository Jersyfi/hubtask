# S-2 — The master key rotation, completed

**2026-09-06, a throwaway PostgreSQL 16 through Testcontainers.** The drill of 2026-09-04
([evidence](./S-2-2026-09-04.md)) proved the roll-forward and stopped, honestly, at the refusal:
nothing in the system could move a value from under an older key, so the count of rows naming it
could never reach zero and step 4 of the procedure had no safe moment. This file records the run
that reached it, once [#368](https://github.com/Jersyfi/hubtask/issues/368) had built what
ADR-0045 decided.

The drill is `TestARotationCompletesWhenTheCensusReachesZero` in
[`test/integration/key_rotation_test.go`](../../test/integration/key_rotation_test.go), beside the
two of 2026-09-04, so the whole procedure is repeatable rather than an afternoon somebody remembers.

| | Value |
|---|---|
| Database | `postgres:16-alpine`, migrated by the suite's own fixtures |
| Stores under test | all five: `account_mfa`, `identity_provider`, `webhook_subscription`, `backup_target`, `automation_rule` — through their real repositories |
| Crypto | `infrastructure/crypto.Envelope` over a real `Keyring`, `crypto/rand` for entropy; `Rewrap` is the new port method |
| Resealers | the five owning services' own, composed by `sealing.RunReseal` the way the queue's handler composes them |
| Rings exercised | `[k1]` → `[k2 k1]` → `[k2]` |
| Runtime | 0.26 s for the completion; 2.4 s for all three drills together |

## What was proved

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| One value in each of the five stores, sealed under `k1` with its owning service's purpose | the census answers `k1: 5` |
| One round under `[k2 k1]`, inside the tenant's transaction | 5 rewrapped, 0 skipped; one `encryption.resealed` entry in the trail |
| The census after the round | `k1: 0` |
| A second round | moves nothing and leaves no entry |
| Every one of the five, read back through its repository and opened under a ring holding `k2` alone | the plaintext that went in, in each case |
| The rule's `version` after its actions were rewritten | unchanged — a rotation is nobody's edit |

The last two rows are the ones worth having. Opening under `[k2]` alone is what "retired" means,
and the drill of 2026-09-04 had shown that the same read answered an unavailability before the
round existed. The unchanged version is the promise `security.md` §8.1 makes to the person editing
their rule at the moment the operator rotates: no conflict they did not cause.

## What was deliberately not proved here

**A round over more than one workspace, and the queue's own path.** The drill runs the round the
way the handler runs it, in one tenant's transaction; the fan-out - one job per workspace through
the control plane's enumerator, deduplicated while pending - is covered by the sealing service's
unit tests, and the SG-3 negatives in
[`sealing_test.go`](../../test/integration/sealing_test.go) prove that no store's re-seal reaches
another workspace's rows. A rotation against the running production namespace is H-10's second
half, and it will be drilled there against real data rather than against a fixture.
