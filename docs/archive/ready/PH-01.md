## 3. Decisions

- [x] D1 **Split check 10.** PH-01 carries "not erased or anonymised but restricted; everything no
  hold covers is erased as usual". What restriction keeps the data out of is a new task, **PH-09**,
  carrying UC-PRV-03 (10) as well: the automation engine and the suggestion service read the
  restriction (UC-PRV-04 checks 4 and 5, findings that block it), the prompt builder treats a
  restricted person's content as an objecting person's (PH-03), and the exports follow the owner's
  answer to O1. Written into `milestone-PH.md` — decided by worker (`docs/backlog/README.md`,
  "Tasks move"): the restriction half needs the owner and a mechanism PH-03 builds; the hold half
  needs neither, so PH-01 is `ready` and nothing is stacked on a waiting task. Its issue is opened
  by the coordinator and cited in the milestone once it exists.
- [x] D2 **What each step of the erasure keeps.** A located row is kept when a hold covers its
  entry: a `TENANT` hold, a `CONTAINER` hold on its hub or collection, an `ITEM` hold on it or an
  entry above it (`Holds.Blocking` with the entry's hub, collection and path). That keeps the
  person's comments in `FULL_DELETE`, their assignments (a field of the held entry) and the entries
  they created. An `ACCOUNT` hold keeps that account's own rows — the entries it created (with
  their fields, so another person's assignment on them), its comments, its attached files, the
  account itself — and nothing else: the erasure removes rows by identifier and has no cascade, so
  a comment the subject wrote on A's entry is not A's and goes. (The purge reaches further, D9,
  because a comment falls with its entry — data-retention.md §4 item 6.) The intake (matched by
  address, no entry, no author) is kept only by a `TENANT` hold, as the intake's sweep is
  (`RunRetention.go:690-705`). Several holds covering one row: the oldest in force is named
  (`ORDER BY placed_at, id`). One pure function in `core/domain/model/privacy` decides it; the
  erasure and the preview both call it — decided by worker; data-protection.md §4.1 rewritten in
  step 18.
- [x] D3 **What no hold reaches.** Credentials (tokens, calendar feeds, sync devices), notifications
  and unattached uploads go in every case — decided by `RunRetention.go:690-695` (a workspace-wide
  hold does not reach the notification history), data-retention.md §3 (sessions and devices swept
  without a hold), data-protection.md §5 (unreferenced media reconciled without one). Only where the
  account itself is kept (D4: a `TENANT` or `ACCOUNT` hold) do its password and sessions stay, so the
  person still signs in (check 13).
- [x] D4 **The account follows checks 3, 6 and 13 as written.** A hold keeps the account itself only
  when it reaches it: an `ACCOUNT` hold on the subject (check 13) or a `TENANT` hold (it covers every
  row of the workspace, the account row included). Then the account keeps everything, is
  `RESTRICTED`, gets no pseudonym, and the case keeps its subject. Under any other hold the account
  is anonymised and pseudonymised exactly as today — *former user*, no address, no password, no
  credential, no sign-in, a pseudonym (checks 3 and 6) — and the kept contributions stay attributed
  to that row, which is what *anonymise* already does with every contribution. `FULL_DELETE` with a
  kept located row anonymises the account instead of deleting it, so the kept rows keep their author
  (§ 1); the remainder deletes it once nothing is kept, and the case clears its subject then
  (`Perform.go:88-93` moves with it). Such kept rows are out of processing already: an anonymised
  author is refused by every reader of the restriction (`Identity.sql:220-229`). Decided by worker:
  a hold reaches what it names — a hub's hold names the hub's content, not a person's identity,
  which belongs to no hub; only the two scopes that name the account keep it. This keeps checks 3, 6,
  10 and 13 as written. Rejected: keeping the whole account for any hold (the first draft — changes
  checks 3, 6, 10 and 13); an attribution stub that keeps the name (changes check 3 under a narrow
  hold); a placeholder account taking over unheld contributions (a second anonymisation mechanism;
  under this decision unheld contributions are anonymised as today anyway).
- [x] D5 **Partly completed is `COMPLETED` with what was kept.** New table `erasure_kept`
  (migration 0120, expand only): tenant, case (`ON DELETE CASCADE`), hold id (no foreign key: a
  restore rewrites `legal_hold` and must not take the record of what was kept with it), whether the
  account was kept, counts of entries, comments, assignments, intake, `recorded_at`, `erased_at`,
  `blocked_code` and `blocked_params` (D20); primary key (tenant, case, hold); a partial index on
  (tenant, hold) where `erased_at IS NULL`; row-level security as every tenant table. **The rows are
  upserted on (tenant, case, hold) inside `Erase`'s main transaction** — the one that took the
  shared hold lock, read the holds and deleted — so a release committed after it finds them and one
  committed before it was seen. The case answers `kept` (per hold: its id and scope, the counts,
  `erased_at` once the rest went, the blocking code while one stands) and `kept_legal_basis:
  ART_17_3_E` while it has rows; the status stays `COMPLETED`. Decided by worker. Rejected: a status
  `PARTLY_COMPLETED` — it changes data-protection.md §4's state machine, the `CHECK`, every
  `Closed()` reader and an enum clients switch on, and the remainder would have to move a closed
  case again (`Request.go:80-83`).
- [x] D6 **The release seeds the remainder; one case, one writer.** `ReleaseLegalHold` gains a port
  its transaction calls (`RemainderSeeder`, declared in the lifecycle service, implemented in the
  privacy service, wired in `cmd/server/main.go`): one job `privacy.erasure_remainder` per case with
  a pending row for that hold, dedupe `dsr-remainder:<case>:<hold>`, in the release's own
  transaction — a committed release always has its jobs. The job
  (`presentation/worker/PrivacyErasureRemainder.go`, detached like `PrivacyRequest`) calls
  `ResumeErasure`, which runs the same erasure against the holds in force, replaces the case's rows
  (released holds' rows get `erased_at`, a hold still keeping takes the rest), assigns the pseudonym
  and deletes the account once nothing keeps it, and writes `dsr.remainder_erased` (counts per
  place, the hold ids, no name). **Both the first erasure and `ResumeErasure` take the case row `FOR
  UPDATE` at the start of their main transaction**; `ResumeErasure` serves a case still
  `IN_PROGRESS` (a release between the erasure's main transaction and the completion) by erasing the
  rest and leaving the completion to `Perform`, whose retry then finds nothing more to keep. As the
  system actor, like `Perform` (`Perform.go:46-48`). Decided by worker. Rejected: one job per release
  walking every case (one case's failure holds up the others); an outbox event (no in-process
  consumer exists).
- [x] D7 **A hold and a deletion never pass each other.** `Active` takes
  `pg_advisory_xact_lock_shared(hashtext('legal_hold:' || current_tenant_id()))` before it reads (an
  advisory lock writes nothing, so it works in the read-only transaction of the rule preview);
  placing and releasing take the exclusive lock as the first statement of their transaction, before
  `Find` or the insert. Every deletion path reads the holds inside the transaction that deletes, so a
  hold placed concurrently either waits for it or is seen by it; readers never wait for each other.
  Decided by worker (known trap "read-then-write without a lock"; precedent `Audit.sql:13`).
- [x] D8 **A kept account's restriction cannot be lifted** while the case has a pending row that
  kept the account: `RestrictProcessing` refuses `privacy.restriction_kept_by_erasure` (409); the
  web app offers no lifting today (UC-PRV-04 *Today*, check 1). The remainder itself is not stopped
  by the restriction its own erasure set (data-retention.md §4 item 2, written in §4.1). Decided by
  worker: lifting it would make held data processable again, which check 10 forbids.
- [x] D9 **Every purge honours an `ACCOUNT` hold, and every held descendant.** `Target` gains
  `Contributors` — the accounts that created an entry, wrote a comment on it or uploaded a file
  attached to it, across what the purge removes — read by one statement per batch, and only while an
  `ACCOUNT` hold is in force; `Blocking` matches an `ACCOUNT` hold against them. The reach is wider
  than the erasure's (D2) because a comment falls with its entry — decided by data-retention.md §4
  item 6. `Subtree` judges every entry it removes, not its root alone; `Purger.Sweep` keeps back a
  trashed entry or container while any descendant is not going in the same pass, as the rule sweep
  does (`RetainedDescendants`, `BlockedByDescendant`, `RetentionSweep.go:332-361`) — the findings of
  § 1, fixed first, tests first, in their own commit. Paths: `Purger.Subtree`, `Purger.Sweep` (empty
  trash, trash pass), `Sweeper.blocked`, the rule preview (`RetentionRules.go:393-402`). Decided by
  worker: LegalHold.go's own rule — a hold stored and not honoured is worse than one refused.
- [x] D10 **A hold names something here.** Placing a `CONTAINER`, `ITEM` or `ACCOUNT` hold reads its
  target under the caller's tenant and refuses `lifecycle.hold_target_not_found` (422, field
  `/scope_id`) when there is none; an anonymised account may be held. The check for the three old
  scopes is a finding, own commit. `ACCOUNT` is accepted only once every path above honours it
  (step 10). Decided by worker.
- [x] D11 **The preview.** Use case `PreviewErasure`, `GET
  /privacy/requests/{requestId}/erasure-preview?mode=`, read-only, `MANAGE_MEMBERS` and
  `privacy:read` as the list, `admin:tenants` for an `INSTALLATION` case; answers per hold what it
  would keep (hold id, scope, counts, whether the account) for the mode asked, else the case's, else
  `ANONYMIZE`. **No reason**: a hold's reason is read under `STRUCTURE` or `READ_CONFIGURATION`

  Decided by worker: a read is a `GET` (api-guidelines.md §2); the retention preview's `POST
  …:preview` takes a body, this takes none. Rejected: answering it on every case in the list.
- [x] D12 **Codes.** New: `lifecycle.hold_target_not_found`, `privacy.not_an_erasure`,
  `privacy.restriction_kept_by_erasure`; `lifecycle.hold_account_scope_unavailable` leaves code and

- [x] D13 **Personal data.** `erasure_kept` holds identifiers and counts, no name, no content: a new
